Most of what I learn in the lab never makes it into a paper. The weird packet capture at 2 a.m., the config flag that took a day to find, the attack that *should* have worked but didn't: all of it usually ends up buried in a terminal history or a notebook nobody else will read.

This log is where those notes go instead.

## What you'll find here

I work at the point where networks meet the physical world, so most posts will fall into one of these buckets:

- **5G RedCap & cellular IoT**: protocol-level weaknesses in reduced-capability devices, and how to measure them on a testbed.
- **ICS / SCADA**: cyber-physical attacks on water distribution and other critical infrastructure, and the detectors that catch them.
- **Privacy-preserving ML**: federated learning, differential privacy and secure aggregation for sharing threat intelligence between cities.
- **Offensive notes**: CTF write-ups, web exploitation and the occasional TryHackMe rabbit hole.

> [!NOTE]
> Everything here is written for defenders and researchers. Attack details stay at the level needed to understand and mitigate a problem, and anything sensitive goes through responsible disclosure first.

## How I'll write

Short when possible, detailed when it matters. Expect lab setups you can reproduce, configs that actually work, and honest notes on what *didn't* work, because that part is usually missing.

> [!PULL]
> If it took me a day to figure out, it deserves ten minutes of writing.

If something here helps you, or if you think I got something wrong, [reach out](../index.html#contact). That's the whole point.
